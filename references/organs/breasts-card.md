# Breasts — quick-reference card

**What they do:** The breasts contain mammary glands that can make milk after childbirth. Small clusters called lobules produce the milk, and tubes called ducts carry it to the nipple. The rest is fatty and supportive tissue. Breasts change with hormones, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and menopause — lumpiness and tenderness that come and go with your cycle are common and usually normal.

**What can go wrong:** Benign lumps — cysts and fibroadenomas (most lumps are NOT cancer) · breast pain linked to the cycle · mastitis (painful infection, usually while breastfeeding) · nipple discharge · breast cancer.

**Doctors:** Primary care doctor (first stop) · breast surgeon · radiologist (imaging and biopsies) · oncologist (if cancer is found) · lactation consultant (breastfeeding support).

**Usual tests:** Clinical breast exam · mammogram · breast ultrasound · breast MRI · core-needle biopsy (the test that actually confirms what a lump is).

**Keep them healthy:**
- Know your own normal — check regularly and report changes
- Attend breast screening offered by your local programme
- Keep a healthy weight and stay physically active
- Limit alcohol

**See a doctor soon (within days):** A new lump or thickening · skin dimpling or an orange-peel texture · a nipple newly turning inward · nipple discharge, especially if bloody · redness or swelling of one breast · a lump in the armpit.
**Emergency — same-day care; call your local emergency number if you can't be seen quickly or feel very unwell:** A hot, painful, red breast with fever or flu-like aching (possible mastitis or abscess — needs treatment today). True breast emergencies are rare; most breast changes need a prompt appointment, not an emergency call.
