# Uterus — quick-reference card

**What it does:** The womb — a muscular, pear-shaped organ where a baby grows during pregnancy. Its lining (the endometrium) builds up each month and sheds as a period; in labour, its strong muscles contract to push the baby out.

**What can go wrong:** Fibroids (benign growths) · endometriosis · adenomyosis · endometrial polyps · heavy menstrual bleeding · uterine prolapse (womb slips down) · endometrial cancer.

**Doctors:** Gynaecologist · gynaecologic oncologist (cancer) · fertility specialist · urogynaecologist (prolapse).

**Usual tests:** Pelvic exam · pelvic/transvaginal ultrasound · hysteroscopy (camera inside the womb) · endometrial biopsy · MRI. Note: a Pap test checks the cervix, not the uterus.

**Keep it healthy:**
- Keep up routine gynaecology check-ups
- Healthy weight and regular activity
- Treat very heavy or painful periods as worth investigating, not "just normal"
- Report any bleeding after menopause promptly

**See a doctor soon:** Very heavy periods · bleeding between periods · pelvic pressure or ongoing pain · periods that suddenly get much worse.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Bleeding that soaks through a pad or tampon every hour for 2 or more hours · severe pelvic pain · any bleeding during pregnancy.
