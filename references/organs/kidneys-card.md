### Quick-reference card: Kidneys

**What they do:** Two bean-shaped organs, one on each side of the spine below the ribs. They filter your blood — removing waste and extra fluid as urine — and help balance salts, minerals, and blood pressure. Each kidney holds about a million tiny filters called nephrons.

**What can go wrong:** Chronic kidney disease (gradual loss of filtering, often from diabetes or high blood pressure) · kidney stones · urinary tract infections that reach the kidney · sudden (acute) kidney injury.

**Doctors:** Nephrologist (medical kidney care) · urologist (surgical — stones, blockages) · primary care doctor (screening, blood pressure and diabetes control).

**Usual tests:** Blood creatinine and eGFR (estimate of filtering ability) · urine test (protein, blood) · blood pressure check · ultrasound · CT scan (stones).

**Keep them healthy:**
- Keep blood pressure and blood sugar in their target ranges
- Drink enough water — pale-yellow urine is a good sign
- Eat less salt; don't smoke; limit alcohol
- Avoid frequent use of NSAID painkillers without medical advice

**See a doctor soon:** Swollen ankles, feet, or face · foamy or bloody urine · urinating much more or much less than usual · persistent tiredness with pale skin.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Sudden severe pain in the side or back below the ribs, especially with vomiting or fever (possible stone with infection) · little or no urine output combined with swelling or breathlessness.
