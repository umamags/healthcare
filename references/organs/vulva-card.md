# Vulva — quick-reference card

**What it does:** The vulva is the external genitals — the labia (folds of skin), the clitoris, and the opening of the vagina. It protects the internal organs and is rich in nerve endings, giving sexual sensation. Many people say "vagina" for the whole area, but the vulva is the part on the outside.

**What can go wrong:** Irritation or allergy from scented products · lichen sclerosus (itchy, pale patches of skin) · Bartholin's cyst or abscess (swelling near the vaginal opening) · genital warts (caused by HPV) · vulvar cancer (rare).

**Doctors:** Gynaecologist · dermatologist (vulvar skin) · vulvar specialist · primary care doctor.

**Usual tests:** Visual exam · swabs (for infection) · skin biopsy (a tiny sample, if skin changes persist).

**Keep it healthy:**
- Wash with plain water only; skip scented products
- Wear breathable cotton underwear
- Learn what your own vulva looks like, so changes stand out
- Use condoms to lower STI risk, including HPV

**See a doctor soon:** Itching or soreness that won't settle · a lump or swelling · skin turning paler, thicker, or changing texture · a sore that doesn't heal.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** A painful swelling near the vaginal opening with fever, rapidly spreading redness, or pain so severe you can't sit (possible Bartholin's abscess needing same-day treatment).
