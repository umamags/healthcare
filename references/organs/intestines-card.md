### Quick-reference card: Intestines

**What they do:** The small intestine — several metres long — absorbs nutrients from food into the blood. The large intestine absorbs water and forms the remaining waste into stool. Trillions of gut bacteria help with digestion and more.

**What can go wrong:** IBS (irritable bowel syndrome) · IBD — Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis (different from IBS!) · coeliac disease · diverticular disease · bowel obstruction · colorectal cancer. (Appendicitis is covered on our appendix page.)

**Doctors:** Gastroenterologist (digestive specialist) · colorectal surgeon · dietitian (works alongside your doctor) · primary care doctor.

**Usual tests:** Colonoscopy (camera through the rectum) · stool tests · blood tests · CT or MRI scans · capsule endoscopy (swallowed camera pill) · biopsy.

**Keep them healthy:**
- Eat plenty of fibre: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes
- Drink enough water; stay active — both keep things moving
- Don't ignore bowel screening where it's offered
- Know your family history of bowel disease or cancer

**See a doctor soon:** Blood in the stool · a lasting change in bowel habits · unexplained weight loss · persistent abdominal pain or bloating.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Sudden severe abdominal pain with swelling and no longer passing gas or stool (possible obstruction) · vomiting blood or black stools · heavy rectal bleeding.
