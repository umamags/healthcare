# Fallopian tubes — quick-reference card

**What they do:** Two slender tubes, one from each ovary to the womb. Their finger-like ends catch the released egg, tiny hairs sweep it along, and fertilisation — egg meeting sperm — usually happens inside the tube.

**What can go wrong:** Blockage (a common cause of infertility) · hydrosalpinx (swollen, fluid-filled tube) · pelvic inflammatory disease (infection of the tubes) · ectopic pregnancy (pregnancy growing in the tube) · tubal cancer (rare).

**Doctors:** Gynaecologist · fertility specialist.

**Usual tests:** Pregnancy test (hCG blood/urine test) · pelvic ultrasound · HSG (dye X-ray that checks the tubes are open) · laparoscopy.

**Keep them healthy:**
- Protect against sexually transmitted infections — use condoms with new partners
- Test for STIs regularly; untreated chlamydia often has no symptoms but can silently damage tubes
- Get pelvic pain, fever, or unusual discharge checked promptly

**See a doctor soon:** Ongoing pelvic pain · unusual discharge or fever · trying to conceive for a year without success (six months if over 35).
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Missed period or positive pregnancy test WITH one-sided pelvic pain · shoulder-tip pain · dizziness or fainting (possible ectopic pregnancy).
