# Prostate — quick-reference card

**What it does:** A walnut-sized gland just under the bladder. It makes seminal fluid that nourishes and carries sperm. It wraps around the urethra (the urine tube) — which is why, when it enlarges, it squeezes the tube and causes urinary symptoms.

**What can go wrong:** Benign prostatic enlargement (BPH — very common with age) · prostatitis (inflammation, sometimes infection) · prostate cancer.

**Doctors:** Urologist · oncologist (cancer care) · primary care doctor (usually the first stop).

**Usual tests:** Symptom review · urine tests · digital rectal exam · PSA blood test (rises with enlargement and infection too — not a cancer test by itself) · ultrasound · prostate MRI · biopsy.

**Keep it healthy:**
- Stay active and keep a healthy weight
- Don't smoke
- Talk to your doctor about new urinary symptoms rather than just accepting them
- Discuss PSA testing with your doctor — the decision is personal

**See a doctor soon:** Weak stream or straining · frequent night-time urination · blood in urine or semen · persistent pelvic or perineal pain.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Complete inability to pass urine · high fever with prostate/pelvic pain and feeling very unwell.
