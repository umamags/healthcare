# Teeth — full article

**What they do.** Your 32 adult teeth cut, tear, and grind food so digestion can begin in the mouth. They shape the sounds of speech and support the structure of your face. Each tooth has three layers: enamel (the hard outer shell), dentin (softer, sensitive middle), and pulp (nerves and blood vessels at the core). Unlike bone, enamel can't repair itself once damaged — which is why prevention matters so much.

**What can go wrong.**
*Common:* Tooth decay — bacteria feed on sugars and produce acid that eats through enamel, forming cavities. Gum disease — starts as gingivitis (red, bleeding gums; reversible) and can progress to periodontitis (infection damages the bone holding teeth; teeth can loosen). Tooth sensitivity — worn enamel or receding gums exposing dentin.
*Less common but serious:* Tooth abscess — a pocket of infection at the root; can spread if ignored. Oral cancer — often starts as a sore, lump, or white/red patch that doesn't heal.

**Which doctors get involved.** A general dentist handles check-ups, fillings, and crowns, and refers you onward. An orthodontist straightens teeth (braces, aligners). An endodontist does root canals. A periodontist treats gum disease. An oral & maxillofacial surgeon handles extractions, wisdom teeth, and jaw surgery. A dental hygienist does professional cleaning and prevention coaching.

**Usual tests.** Clinical exam (visual + tapping/probing teeth) · bitewing X-rays (decay between teeth) · panoramic X-ray (whole jaw, wisdom teeth) · pulp vitality/cold test (is the nerve alive?) · periodontal probing (measuring gum-pocket depth).

**Keeping them healthy.** Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, two minutes each time. Clean between teeth daily — a brush can't reach there. Cut down sugary and acidic drinks (including fruit juice and fizzy drinks). Don't use teeth as tools. Wear a mouthguard for contact sports. See a dentist regularly even when nothing hurts — most dental disease is painless until it's advanced.
*Myth box:* "Brushing harder cleans better." False — hard brushing wears enamel and pushes gums back. Gentle, thorough brushing wins.

**Warning signs.** See a dentist soon: toothache lasting more than a day or two, pain on hot/cold that lingers, bleeding gums, a loose adult tooth, a mouth sore or patch not healed in 2 weeks. These signs don't diagnose anything by themselves — they mean it's time to talk to a dentist. Emergency — call your local emergency number now: facial or neck swelling with fever, difficulty swallowing or breathing, bleeding that won't stop, or a knocked-out adult tooth — keep it moist (in milk, or in your cheek) and reach a dentist within the hour; reimplantation is often possible.

**How doctors reach a diagnosis.** Symptoms and history → visual exam and probing → X-rays to see what's hidden → pulp tests if the nerve is suspect → diagnosis and a treatment plan. Most dental diagnoses are made in a single visit.

**Common treatment approaches.** Fillings for cavities · root canal to save an infected tooth · crowns to rebuild weakened teeth · extraction when a tooth can't be saved · implants, bridges, or dentures to replace missing teeth · scaling and root planing for gum disease · braces or aligners for alignment. Antibiotics may calm an abscess but don't fix its cause — the tooth still needs treatment.
