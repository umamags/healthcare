# Scrotum — quick-reference card

**What it does:** The scrotum is the loose sac of skin and muscle behind the penis that holds the two testes. Sperm production works best slightly below body temperature, so muscles in the scrotal wall pull the testes closer to the body when it's cold and let them hang lower when it's warm. Most scrotal problems actually come from the structures inside the sac — the testes, epididymis, veins, or a hernia — rather than the skin itself.

**What can go wrong:** Hydrocele (fluid collecting around a testis) · varicocele (enlarged veins, often described as a "bag of worms") · epididymal cyst / spermatocele (a small fluid-filled lump) · inguinal hernia bulging down into the scrotum · skin cysts and irritation · Fournier's gangrene (rare, severe infection of the skin).

**Doctors:** Primary care doctor (usually first) · urologist · general surgeon (for hernias).

**Usual tests:** Physical exam (standing and lying down) · scrotal ultrasound · urine tests · blood tests.

**Keep it healthy:**
- Wear supportive underwear, and a protective cup for contact sports
- Keep the skin clean and dry; get persistent irritation checked
- Know what your scrotum normally feels like — and report new lumps, swelling, or heaviness

**See a doctor soon:** A painless lump or swelling · heaviness or a dragging ache · one side slowly growing larger · skin changes, itching, or sores that persist.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Sudden severe pain in the scrotum (possible twisted testis — see the testes page) · a painful groin or scrotal bulge with nausea or vomiting (possible trapped hernia) · rapidly spreading redness with fever and severe pain.
