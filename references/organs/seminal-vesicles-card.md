# Seminal Vesicles — quick-reference card

**What they do:** The seminal vesicles are a pair of small, coiled glands tucked behind the bladder. They produce most of the fluid volume of semen — an alkaline, sugar-rich fluid (the sugar is fructose, which feeds and fuels the sperm). On each side, the duct of the seminal vesicle joins the sperm duct (vas deferens) to form an ejaculatory duct passing through the prostate.

**What can go wrong:** Seminal vesiculitis (inflammation, often alongside prostate inflammation) · cysts · small stones (calculi) · being absent from birth — linked with absent sperm ducts and infertility · cancer (very rare).

**Doctors:** Urologist · fertility specialist / andrologist.

**Usual tests:** Semen analysis (volume and fructose) · transrectal ultrasound · pelvic MRI · urine and semen cultures · prostate exam.

**Keep them healthy:**
- There is little that targets these glands specifically — general steps help most
- Get urinary and prostate infections treated; don't ignore them
- Practise safer sex to reduce infection risk
- Manage diabetes, which raises the risk of infections

**See a doctor soon:** Blood in the semen · painful ejaculation · deep pelvic or perineal pain · infertility, or very low semen volume.
**Emergency — true emergencies from the seminal vesicles are rare; seek emergency care (call your local emergency number) for:** High fever with pelvic pain and feeling very unwell · being unable to pass urine at all.
