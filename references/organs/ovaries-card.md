# Ovaries — quick-reference card

**What they do:** Two small glands, one on each side of the womb. They store a lifetime supply of eggs, release one roughly each month (ovulation), and make the hormones estrogen and progesterone that run the menstrual cycle and support pregnancy.

**What can go wrong:** Functional cysts (common, usually harmless) · PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) · endometriosis cysts (endometriomas) · ovarian torsion (twisted ovary) · premature ovarian insufficiency (early menopause) · ovarian cancer.

**Doctors:** Gynaecologist · reproductive endocrinologist (fertility, PCOS) · gynaecologic oncologist (cancer).

**Usual tests:** Pelvic ultrasound · hormone blood tests · CA-125 blood test (a marker, not a screening test) · CT/MRI · laparoscopy.

**Keep them healthy:**
- Routine gynaecology check-ups
- Healthy weight and regular activity support hormone balance
- Know your family history — ovarian and breast cancer can run in families
- Report bloating or pelvic pain that doesn't settle — there is no reliable screening test for ovarian cancer

**See a doctor soon:** Bloating most days for weeks · feeling full quickly when eating · ongoing pelvic or tummy pain · needing to urinate urgently or more often.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Sudden severe one-sided pelvic pain · pelvic pain with nausea, vomiting, or fainting (possible twisted ovary or ruptured cyst).
