# Eyes — quick-reference card

**What they do:** Capture light and turn it into signals the brain reads as vision. The cornea and lens focus light onto the retina, which converts it; the optic nerve carries the message to the brain.

**What can go wrong:** Refractive errors (short-sightedness, long-sightedness, astigmatism) · cataract (cloudy lens) · glaucoma (optic-nerve damage, often with high eye pressure) · age-related macular degeneration · diabetic retinopathy · conjunctivitis ("pink eye") · dry eye.

**Doctors:** Ophthalmologist (a medical doctor — diagnoses, prescribes, and operates) · optometrist (vision testing, glasses and contact lenses; exact scope varies by country) · optician (fits glasses).

**Usual tests:** Visual acuity chart · refraction test · slit-lamp exam · eye pressure check (tonometry) · dilated retinal exam · OCT scan (detailed retina imaging).

**Keep them healthy:**
- Regular eye exams — especially with diabetes or a family history of glaucoma
- Sunglasses with UV protection
- The 20-20-20 rule for screens: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds
- Don't smoke; keep blood sugar and blood pressure controlled

**See a doctor soon:** Gradual vision changes · persistent dryness, itching, or irritation · a big increase in floaters or flashes of light.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now / seek emergency care:** Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes · an eye injury or chemical splash (rinse with clean water right away, then get emergency care) · a dark curtain or shadow spreading across your vision (possible retinal detachment).
