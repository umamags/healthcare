# Vagina — quick-reference card

**What it does:** A muscular passage connecting the uterus to the outside of the body. Menstrual blood flows out through it, and during childbirth it stretches to become the birth canal. It is largely self-cleaning: healthy bacteria keep it balanced and mildly acidic.

**What can go wrong:** Yeast infection (thrush) · bacterial vaginosis · sexually transmitted infections (e.g. trichomoniasis, chlamydia) · dryness and thinning around menopause · pelvic organ prolapse.

**Doctors:** Gynaecologist · primary care doctor · pelvic floor physiotherapist (support for prolapse and pelvic pain).

**Usual tests:** Pelvic exam · vaginal swabs · vaginal pH test · STI tests · urine test.

**Keep it healthy:**
- Never douche — the vagina cleans itself
- Use condoms to lower STI risk
- Avoid scented soaps and "feminine hygiene" sprays inside
- Wipe front to back; wear breathable underwear

**See a doctor soon:** Discharge that changes in colour, amount, or smell · itching or burning · pain during sex · sores or blisters.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Heavy vaginal bleeding · severe pelvic pain with fever · high fever, rash, and dizziness/fainting while using a tampon (possible toxic shock syndrome — remove the tampon and get help at once).
