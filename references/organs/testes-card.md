# Testes — quick-reference card

**What they do:** The two testicles make sperm and testosterone (the main male hormone). Sperm production works best slightly cooler than core body temperature — which is why the testes hang outside the body in the scrotum.

**What can go wrong:** Testicular torsion (twisted cord — emergency) · epididymitis/orchitis (inflammation, often infection) · varicocele (enlarged veins) · hydrocele (fluid around the testicle) · testicular cancer (commonest in younger men) · low testosterone · undescended testicle (present from birth).

**Doctors:** Urologist · andrologist (male fertility & hormones) · uro-oncologist (cancer) · your primary care doctor usually refers.

**Usual tests:** Physical exam · scrotal Doppler ultrasound · urine and STI tests · tumour marker blood tests (AFP, hCG, LDH) if cancer is suspected · semen analysis · testosterone blood test.

**Keep them healthy:**
- Know what feels normal for you, and report any change
- Wear protection for contact sports
- Practise safer sex to reduce infection risk
- Seek prompt care for any scrotal pain or swelling

**See a doctor soon:** A new lump or swelling · a dull ache or heaviness · one testicle changing size or firmness.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Sudden severe testicular pain or swelling, especially with nausea — treat as torsion until proven otherwise; hours matter, go immediately.
