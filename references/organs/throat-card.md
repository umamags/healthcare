# Throat — quick-reference card

**What it does:** A shared crossroads — the pharynx carries food to the esophagus, while the larynx (voice box) carries air to the windpipe and houses the vocal cords. The tonsils help fight infection, especially in childhood.

**What can go wrong:** Sore throat (mostly viral) · tonsillitis · strep throat (bacterial) · laryngitis (hoarseness or voice loss) · acid reflux irritating the throat · swallowing difficulty (dysphagia) · persistent hoarseness or a neck lump (rarer — needs checking).

**Doctors:** ENT specialist (otolaryngologist) · primary care doctor (simple sore throats) · speech therapist (voice and swallowing rehabilitation).

**Usual tests:** Throat exam · rapid strep test / throat swab · laryngoscopy (a thin camera views the vocal cords) · swallowing assessment.

**Keep it healthy:**
- Don't smoke; limit heavy alcohol use
- Avoid shouting or straining your voice for long periods
- Stay hydrated; treat acid reflux
- Wash hands often — most sore throats are viral

**See a doctor soon:** Sore throat lasting more than a week · hoarseness lasting more than 2–3 weeks · difficulty swallowing · a lump in the neck · recurring tonsillitis.
**Emergency — call your local emergency number now:** Trouble breathing or swallowing · a young child drooling with fever and difficulty swallowing (possible epiglottitis — don't try to look down the throat) · sudden severe throat swelling, e.g. after a sting or with allergy symptoms.
