# Doctor review pack — organs section (6 complex pages)

**Purpose.** The six most medically sensitive pages of the ai-lab.in/healthcare organs section were flagged during drafting as needing a clinician's review before publishing. This pack bundles everything the reviewing doctor needs: scope, a status table, and a targeted checklist per page (article + its paired quick-reference card, quiz, and disclaimer).

**How these pages are written.** Audience: global, non-medical readers. Format: plain-language quick-reference card that expands into a full article with 8 sections — what it does, what can go wrong, which doctors get involved, usual tests, keeping it healthy (plus a myth box), warning signs, how doctors reach a diagnosis, common treatment approaches — one quiz per organ, and a disclaimer ("Educational content — not medical advice").

**What the doctor is being asked.** (1) Factual accuracy of each medical claim in plain language. (2) Whether the warning-signs / emergency guidance is safe and complete for a global audience. (3) Whether any claim is region-specific (drug names, screening ages, emergency numbers were deliberately kept generic — please confirm this holds). (4) Anything missing that a lay reader must not get wrong. Not asked: a rewrite of the tone or structure, or advice on any individual's health.

**Cross-cutting checks (all pages).**
- [ ] Every quiz has exactly one correct answer, and the marked answer is correct.
- [ ] "Last reviewed: [date]" placeholders → fill in the review date on approval.
- [ ] Cross-links ("Related organs", "See also: the cancer section") point to real pages.
- [ ] Emergency guidance uses "call your local emergency number" throughout (no 911/112 hard-coded) — confirm appropriate.
- [ ] No brand-name drugs; only drug classes (statins, inhalers, antivirals, etc.) — confirm the level of detail is safe.
